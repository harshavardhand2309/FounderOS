Lvl-Up Sports · Build specification

What each of our three apps must do

We are building three apps on one platform: Player, Coach, and Venue & Organiser. Cameras installed at partner venues record matches and turn them into stats and video automatically. This document lists every feature each app needs, says how important each one is, and flags the rules that are not negotiable. It is written for the development team — read section 0 first.

Section 00

How to read this

Every feature has an ID, a one-line description of what it does for the person using it, and a priority. Quote the ID in tickets and questions so we are always talking about the same thing.

P0 Launch

The app cannot ship without it. Either the product does not function, or we break a law or an app-store rule.

P1 Fast follow

Within two or three releases after launch. This is where most of our advantage over competitors lives.

P2 Later

Real value, but only worth building once the core loop is proven. Do not let these into the launch scope.

Feature IDs

PF-## platform (built once, used by all three apps) · PL-## Player app · CO-## Coach app · VO-## Venue & Organiser app.

The one rule that is never negotiable

We sell performance measurement, not health assessment. Nothing we build may describe, imply or score a person's physical condition. Words like injury risk, biomechanics, joint load, fatigue and recovery are banned everywhere — screen text, notifications, reports, API field names, database columns and analytics events included, because internal names leak into the interface.

Anything marked CLAIM RISK in this document is a place where a reasonable developer would drift into that language without meaning to. Section 05 gives the full word list and approved replacements, and asks for an automated check in the build pipeline.

One platform, three front doors

A person signs in once with their phone number and gets the app matching their role. Venue operators and tournament organisers share an app because they are usually the same person and both jobs run off the same courts, calendar and cameras. Build the permission model first — it cannot be retrofitted onto a shipped product without a rewrite.

The bystander problem — solve it with the camera, not with paperwork

A fixed court camera sees people who never signed anything: spectators, players on the next court, children, staff. We cannot collect a tap from all of them, so the controls are technical: frame only the court (masked field of view, audited as a device setting), blur any person not part of the session, keep audio off by default, and give anyone a "remove me from this footage" route that works without an account. Venues must display signage at the entrance and courtside — make a photo of the installed signage a gate before the camera goes live.

Section 02

Player app

The player brings nothing to the court — no tripod, no phone mount, no charging. They tap in, play, and walk off with video and numbers. Everything below serves one repeating loop, and the loop is what we are actually building.

The loop

Book a camera court → tap in at the court → play → the recording finds me automatically → stats and clips ready in minutes → share a clip → my rating moves → book again.

Roughly half of that chain is plumbing rather than features, and it is the half most easily left out: checking in, controlling the recording, knowing which body on court is which account, fixing a wrong score, and showing that processing is under way. Without those, the stats either never appear or appear against the wrong person.

Section 03

Coach app

A coach's day is: who is turning up, what am I running today, who owes me money, what do I tell the parent, and did last week's work stick. An app that only answers "here are their numbers" is a report card, and report cards get opened once a month. This app has to be the coach's daily home screen.

The launch loop

The coach opens the app and sees today's sessions. They tap a batch and mark attendance in one tap, offline. The batch view flags three players improving and two slipping. They tap a player: recent form, three strengths, three things to work on. They tap a weakness and get a video playlist of exactly those points. They draw on one frame, record a ten-second voice note, send it. They broadcast "6am cancelled, rain" to the batch. That is P0. Everything else is expansion.

Venue & Organiser app

One app for two jobs, because they are usually the same person and both run off the same courts, calendar and cameras. Inside, keep the two clearly separated: a space for running the venue and a space for running events. Build the operations layer before the insights layer

Almost every number a venue wants — players, coaches, hours used, revenue, best players, most improved — is derived from bookings, memberships and recordings. Section 05

Language rules — read before writing any screen text

We measure performance. We do not assess health. That distinction is what keeps our software out of medical-device regulation, and it is decided by the words on our screens — because in Indian law a product's intended purpose is judged by what it claims. A single label reading "injury risk" is evidence of intent.

Why a developer will get this wrong without meaning to

Every pose-estimation library, research paper and vendor datasheet the movement module will be built from is written in exactly this vocabulary. The default is to copy it. So is the default for translators, for anyone writing a push notification, and for any AI that generates insight text. This cannot be enforced by good intentions — it needs a build-time check.

Banned words, everywhere

Not just on screen: also in notification and WhatsApp templates, PDF reports, store listings and screenshots, API field names, database columns and analytics event names — because internal names leak into the interface sooner or later.

GroupNever use
The core listinjury risk · injury prevention · biomechanics · asymmetry · prognosis · screening · joint load
Clinical wordsdiagnose · diagnosis · treat · treatment · therapy · rehab · rehabilitation · clinical · medical · physio · doctor · screening (use "checking" where you mean content moderation)
Body-condition wordsstrain · sprain · overuse · muscle imbalance · posture correction · tendon · ligament · joint
Physiology-adjacentfatigue · recovery · readiness · training load · load management · calories burned · intensity score · wellness score · health score · fitness assessment
Claim phrasing"reduces your risk of…" · "prevents…" · "safe technique" · "unsafe technique" · "protects your…" · "below normal"

Say this instead

Where a developer reaches forUse
Biomechanics, biomechanical analysisTechnique analysis · motion analysis
Asymmetry, imbalance between sidesShot-side consistency · left/right shot distribution
Joint load, strain, injury risk scoreNo equivalent — do not build the feature
Training load, fatigueCourt time · session volume · distance covered · shots played
Drop-off late in a matchLate-match intensity · change in work rate across sets
Recovery, readinessNo equivalent — do not build it

"Your form is unsafe""Your contact point varied more than usual this session"
"Reduce your injury risk""Build a more repeatable motion"
"Below normal for their age""Below the typical range for this level and age band"

The seven places this will go wrong

  1. Movement and technique screens (PL-26 to PL-30). Highest risk. Every string here needs review, and joint-angle numbers must not be shown at all in version one.
  2. AI-generated insights (PL-56). "Rest more" or "warm up before serving" is one prompt slip away. Constrain generation to a fixed reviewed catalogue and filter the output server-side before it reaches a phone.
  3. The progress dashboard (PL-58). After "you played 14 hours this month" the obvious next sentence is "are you overtraining?" Report volume as fact; never characterise it as too much, too little, risky or safe.
  4. Parent reports (CO-48). Auto-generated sentences drift fastest here. Lock the templates; never suggest seeking or avoiding any physical intervention.
  5. Gear recommendations (PL-67, CO-57). Manufacturer copy is saturated with support, cushioning and protection claims. Filter any third-party product text before display.
  6. Translations (PF-45). A translator will helpfully render "movement efficiency" into something health-flavoured. Run the same check against every language file, not just English.
  7. Junior players. Movement analysis on children draws the most scrutiny of all. For under-16 accounts, show the video overlay only — no scores, no recommendations attached.

How we enforce it

  • One shared dictionary in the codebase mapping every metric to its approved display name, unit and description. Nothing user-facing is written by hand outside it.
  • A build-time check that fails the build if any banned term appears in any language file, notification template, report template, API field name or store metadata file.
  • A server-side filter on every AI-generated string before it reaches a device.
  • A permanent line on every movement screen: "Performance estimate based on video. Not a health, fitness or medical assessment."
  • The same rule applies to the website, sales decks and investor material. One "prevent injuries" line anywhere undoes all of the above.
Section 06

Build order

Some of this is sequencing preference. Some of it is not: the permission model, the consent system and the entitlement service cannot be added to a shipped product without a rewrite, so they come first regardless of how unglamorous they look on a roadmap.

Phase 1 — foundations before any app screen

  • Identity, roles and the data-access grant model (PF-01 to PF-09)
  • Consent ledger, retention rules and the deletion pipeline (PF-10 to PF-20)
  • Venue, court and camera registry with QR binding (PF-06)
  • Entitlement service and the two separate payment catalogues (PF-30 to PF-32)
  • The banned-word build check (Section 05)

Phase 2 — make one venue work end to end

Pick a single venue and make the whole loop real there before building breadth.

  • Venue: courts, bookings, pricing, payments with GST, staff permissions, camera health, consent and signage (VO-01 to VO-05, VO-10 to VO-22, VO-26, VO-34 to VO-39, VO-58 to VO-64)
  • Player: sign-up, find and book a camera court, check in, control the recording, say who is where, confirm the score, get stats and video, share a clip (PL-01 to PL-22, PL-31 to PL-38)
  • Platform: edge recording, rally-only upload, adaptive playback, clip generation, notifications (PF-21 to PF-29, PF-38 to PF-42)

Phase 3 — the coach, and the reasons to come back

  • Coach: the full P0 loop — permissions, roster and batches, attendance, batch dashboard, video with drawing and voice notes, broadcast messaging (CO-01 to CO-29, CO-38 to CO-45)
  • Player: rating with reliability, head-to-head, leaderboards, open matches, trends and goals (PL-39 to PL-44, PL-51, PL-58 to PL-61)
  • Venue: memberships, waitlists, no-show rules, utilisation heatmap and the analytics set (VO-23 to VO-33, VO-42 to VO-51)

Phase 4 — events, and everything that makes money

  • Tournaments end to end, on real court inventory, with live streaming and automatic scoreboards (VO-65 to VO-87)
  • Leagues and ladders — the recurring-booking engine (VO-93 to VO-96)
  • Sponsors and exposure reporting (VO-88 to VO-90)
  • Coach fee collection and private lessons (CO-50 to CO-55)
  • Movement and technique analysis, with the language rules enforced (PL-26 to PL-30)

Phase 5 — later

Marketplace discovery for coaches, the shop, paid streams, multi-venue chain tooling, reels feed, gamification and every other P2 in this document. None of these should be allowed into launch scope.

Section 07

Decisions we owe you

These are ours to answer, not the development team's, and several of them change the architecture. We should settle them before Phase 1 starts.

  1. Three separate apps in the stores, or one app that switches by role? Three listings are clearer to users, but Apple rejects near-duplicate apps — each would need genuinely distinct screens, screenshots and descriptions.
  2. Who is responsible for the footage — us or the venue? Decides who answers a deletion request, what goes in the venue contract, and how access rules are written.
  3. Which of our revenue lines are digital subscriptions? Those must be billed by Apple and Google at their commission. Court bookings, coaching and physical goods must not be. This is 15–30% of gross margin.
  4. Do we ever use customer video to train our models? If yes it needs its own separate opt-in and its own notice — and a defensible answer for footage containing people who never consented.
  5. How long do we keep video on the free tier? Simultaneously our biggest cloud cost, our main reason to upgrade, and a mandatory disclosure in the privacy notice.
  6. Face recognition — yes or no? Recommendation: no. Identify players by court position and tracking instead. If yes, it becomes the most sensitive data we hold and needs its own consent, retention and deletion path.
  7. Do we accept under-13s at all? Recommendation: no. Set the floor at 13 with guardian-managed profiles for 13–17, which keeps us out of the heavier children's-app regimes in both stores.
  8. Does the Player app have public social features at launch? Public posts and comments make us an intermediary under Indian IT rules, with a 24-hour acknowledgement and 7-day resolution duty. Deferring the public social layer materially reduces launch compliance load.
  9. Do we collect players' money, or does the venue? If we collect and deduct commission we need split settlement, payee KYC and possibly tax-deduction obligations. It changes the entire money model.
  10. What do we promise on speed? "Stats ready within X minutes" is the product promise and it should be a number we choose deliberately, then measure ourselves against.

What this document is not

It is a specification of what each app must do and in what order, not how to build it. Screen designs, data models, API contracts and platform choices are the development team's to propose. Where we have stated a mechanism — edge recording, rally-only upload, three privacy levels, camera-verified ratings — it is because the product or the law depends on it, and those we would want to discuss before changing.
